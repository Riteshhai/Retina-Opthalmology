# Capacity limits and second-view acquisition in retinal screening research

**QSART-X manuscript draft · 16 November 2026**

## Abstract

**Objective.** Evaluate how a frozen diabetic retinopathy (DR) policy uses specialist-review and recapture capacity, and whether initial-view information can rank the value of acquiring a second recorded retinal field.

**Design.** We evaluated the fixed QSART dual-budget policy on 396 photographs from 99 patients in DeepDRiD Set-C, a held-out split from the same release. In a separate Set-A development analysis, we fitted a ridge value estimator on 424 eyes from 212 patients and evaluated acquisition on 84 eyes from 42 different patients. We compared policies at equal realized workloads and resampled complete patients for paired intervals.

**Results.** The deployed ordinal policy required specialist review for every Set-C photograph. It exceeded the 30%, 60%, and 90% image review caps and recommended review for all 99 patients at the 100% cap. All 12 policy pairs that matched image and patient review and recapture counts made identical decisions. In the acquisition analysis, the value ranking reduced missed referable eyes from 8 to 6 at eight additional views. All 16 equal-acquisition cost intervals included zero. The acquisition rule based on predicted value above cost used four views without reducing missed referrals.

**Conclusion.** On Set-C, full ordinal coverage coincided with zero automatic clearance and a requirement for full review capacity. A low-budget acquisition benefit was observed on development patients. The equal-workload comparisons leave comparative acquisition benefit uncertain. [Numerical evidence and denominators](QSART_X_EVIDENCE_TABLE.md).

## Introduction

A screening evaluation can report both discrimination against released grades and the workload created by deferral. In QSART, the three possible recommendations are provisional automatic non-referable disposition, image recapture, and specialist review. Recapture and review draw on separate resources. QSART-X extends this evaluation with a held-out patient-level replay and a second-field acquisition analysis. The research questions are whether constrained allocation changes decisions at comparable realized workloads, and whether first-field features identify eyes that benefit from a second recorded field. DeepDRiD supplies eye-level DR grades, image-level quality annotations, and fields centered on different parts of the same eye. [DeepDRiD data paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2022.100512) · [Set-C protocol](SET_C_VALIDATION_V1_PROTOCOL.md) · [Acquisition protocol](PAIRED_VIEW_V1_PROTOCOL.md).

## Materials and methods

### Cohorts and reference labels

The regular-fundus Set-A development manifest contains 848 fitting photographs from 212 patients, 168 selection photographs from 42 patients, and 168 calibration photographs from 42 patients. Each included patient contributes both eyes and both recorded fields. The previously analyzed Set-B confirmation is historical internal evidence. The Set-C readiness audit checked the original release against the pinned author tree and found a cross-split retinal fingerprint candidate. The protocol excluded all four photographs from that patient before scoring. The remaining 396 photographs represent 99 patients. The reference patient grade is the worse released eye grade. Grades 2–4 are referable and grades 3–4 are severe under these study definitions. [Development roster](../../reports/development_v1/data_summary.json) · [Set-C readiness audit and attribution](DEEPDRID_ONLINE_READINESS_V1_RESULTS.md).

The Set-C protocol fixed the model, calibration, gates, and budgets before the first Set-C score. The paired-view study reused only Set-A fitting and selection patients. The value estimator used fitting patients, and its evaluation used different patients who previously contributed to selection of the frozen classifier. Set-A calibration patients supplied no paired-view outcomes. Set-B, Set-C, the reserved APTOS test, and the previously inspected external cohorts supplied no fitting or evaluation samples for the paired-view experiment. [Set-C protocol](SET_C_VALIDATION_V1_PROTOCOL.md) · [Paired-view protocol](PAIRED_VIEW_V1_PROTOCOL.md).

### Frozen predictions and uncertainty

Both experiments used the frozen first-pass MobileNetV3-Small DR and quality checkpoints with the existing preprocessing and production temperatures. Temperature scaling divides the logits by a positive scalar before softmax. We computed referable and severe scores by summing the corresponding grade probabilities. The Set-C conformal diagnostic also used a temperature estimated in a dedicated group of 21 calibration patients and fitted thresholds on a separate group of 21 calibration patients. For the ordinal and least-ambiguous classification (LAC) sets, threshold calibration used the maximum nonconformity over views within a patient. The nominal setting was `alpha = .10`. We measured empirical inclusion per photograph and simultaneous inclusion of all four views per patient. [Temperature scaling](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) · [Set-C provenance and prediction metrics](../../reports/set_c_validation_v1/evaluation.json).

### Capacity-constrained recommendations

Each photograph receives one of the three actions where the constraints admit a feasible allocation. The policy applies separate integer caps, `floor(B_review × N)` and `floor(B_recapture × N)`. The fixed review guards use predicted grade, referable and severe probability, predictive entropy for adequate-quality photographs, and the optional prediction set. In the ordinal and LAC conditions, a set containing a referable grade or an empty set triggers review. Poor-quality photographs without a mandatory review trigger are eligible for recapture or review in the dual-budget arm. The review-only comparator sends all poor-quality photographs to review. Infeasible capacities produce an explicit overload. Eight guarded methods compare how different allocation objectives distribute feasible actions. The photo-level optimizer uses predicted risks. Released grades enter the retrospective outcome ledger. [Allocator](../../qsart/triage_v2.py) · [Locked policy and budget settings](../../reports/set_c_validation_v1/evaluation.json).

Patient rollup assigns specialist review if any view requires it, then recapture if any remaining view requires it. A provisional full-patient automatic recommendation requires four automatic photo recommendations. The full-clearance miss rate is the fraction of fully automatic patients with a referable reference grade. An empty automatic denominator makes that rate undefined. The prespecified harm weights are `(0, 0, 3, 6, 10)` for true grades 0–4. A separate patient-capacity diagnostic computes feasibility using the pointwise mandatory recommendations and counts patients as the capacity unit. Comparative intervals require both arms to match realized review and recapture counts at image and patient level. [Set-C protocol and analysis units](SET_C_VALIDATION_V1_PROTOCOL.md).

### Recorded second-field acquisition

Filename suffix `1` defines the first recorded field and suffix `2` defines the second for this experiment. When selected, the second field contributes to an arithmetic mean of two probability vectors. The target for value estimation is the signed decrease in the retrospective ledger after observing both fields, before acquisition cost. The ledger weights grade-specific missed referrals and charges .25 per referable prediction as a review-workload proxy. A fitted ridge model uses only first-field poor-quality probability, normalized entropy, referable probability, severe probability, and the indicator for a referable predicted grade. We charged .20 per selected second field and included a capped rule that acquires only when estimated gross value exceeds that cost. [Acquisition protocol and feature construction](PAIRED_VIEW_V1_PROTOCOL.md) · [Fitted coefficients and cost results](../../reports/paired_view_v1/evaluation.json).

The prespecified extra-field budgets were 0, 8, 21, 42, 63, and 84 slots. At each interior budget, estimated value was compared with rankings based on poor quality, entropy, referable probability, and a seeded random ordering at the same number of acquisitions. The first-field-only and always-acquire conditions anchor the range. The acquisition study measures review-proxy counts without assigning specialist-review slots. For both principal studies, paired 95% percentile intervals use 2,000 whole-patient resamples with the original allocations fixed. The intervals describe differences conditional on these decisions and cohorts. [Acquisition protocol](PAIRED_VIEW_V1_PROTOCOL.md) · [Set-C protocol](SET_C_VALIDATION_V1_PROTOCOL.md).

## Results

### Classification, calibration, and workload on Set-C

Photo-level five-grade accuracy was 242/396 (61.1%), with macro-F1 0.511 and quadratic weighted kappa 0.715. Worst-predicted-view patient agreement was 52/99 (52.5%). The patient referability endpoint detected 45/48 patients and flagged 15/51 non-referable patients. The AUROC of maximum photo-level referable probability within a patient was 0.956. Image-quality accuracy was 299/396 (75.5%) at the fixed threshold. Frozen temperature scaling reduced photo log loss from 1.067 to 1.022. [Exact fields and denominators](QSART_X_EVIDENCE_TABLE.md#set-c-predictions).

The ordinal set contained all five grades on every photograph, yielding all-view inclusion for all 99 patients. LAC achieved all-view inclusion for 89/99 patients with a mean set size of 3.43. It covered 16/20 grade-4 photographs. Both gates overloaded at the 30%, 60%, and 90% review caps in the dual-budget policy. At full image review capacity they assigned all 99 patients to specialist review, with an undefined conditional clearance miss rate. [Set sizes and capacity results](QSART_X_EVIDENCE_TABLE.md#set-c-policy-and-workload) · [Patient dispositions figure](../../reports/set_c_validation_v1/patient_dispositions.png).

The no-conformal diagnostic at the 60% review and 40% recapture image caps assigned 45 automatic, 114 recapture, and 237 review photo recommendations. The 45 automatic recommendations included four referable photographs, one in the severe subgroup. The recommendations summarized to 74 specialist reviews, 25 recaptures, and zero fully automatic patient dispositions. This allocation fit both image caps and exceeded the corresponding review capacity of 59 patients. [Photo counts, severe misses, and patient capacities](QSART_X_EVIDENCE_TABLE.md#set-c-policy-and-workload).

Across all modes, budget pairs and methods, 39/96 allocation scenarios were feasible and 57 overloaded. The maximum number of patients fully cleared automatically in any feasible scenario was one. Of 84 policy comparisons, 50 had overload in at least one arm, 22 had unequal realized workloads, and 12 matched both workloads at both units. All 12 matched comparisons produced identical image actions and zero observed paired differences. [Audit and comparisons](QSART_X_EVIDENCE_TABLE.md#set-c-policy-and-workload).

### Same-eye acquisition on development patients

On 84 selection eyes, first-view prediction missed eight referable eyes including four severe eyes. At eight additional fields, the value ranking missed six referable eyes including three severe eyes. Review-proxy predictions increased from 36 to 38, and the mean ledger decreased from 0.5833 to 0.4536 per eye. The first-view and eight-field conditions both missed one patient in the severe subgroup. At the same eight-field acquisition count, the ledger difference for value minus entropy was −0.1161 per eye, with a paired 95% interval of [−0.3530, +0.0095]. At 21 and 42 added fields, entropy had the lower observed ledger. All 16 matched cost intervals included zero. The positive-value cutoff acquired four fields and retained eight missed referable eyes, raising the ledger to 0.5929 per eye. [Outcome and interval pointers](QSART_X_EVIDENCE_TABLE.md#paired-view-acquisition) · [Workload figure](../../reports/paired_view_v1/acquisition_workload.png).

## Discussion and limitations

Patient-level discrimination and successful deployment answer different questions from constrained screening utility. The frozen ordinal gate covered every supplied grade by returning every label. At lower review caps that behavior made allocation infeasible, and at full capacity it provided no automatic disposition. The no-conformal diagnostic makes the trade-off concrete. Its image allocation assigned some referable views to the automatic action while its patient rollup required more review slots than the corresponding patient budget allowed. This distinction matters when a cohort contains multiple views of each patient.

The observed acquisition ranking improved the eight-field proxy ledger relative to first-view prediction. Comparisons against competing policies at the same acquisition count remain uncertain. The capped value threshold added work without reducing measured misses. Classifier fitting and value-model fitting shared training patients, and acquisition evaluation reuses the classifier selection patients. The same-release Set-C split, unresolved possibility of undetected overlap, small severe strata, and correlated views limit interpretation. The patient bootstrap conditions on the fitted models and measured assignments.

Released reference grades are the measured outcome. There were no observed completed recaptures, clinician review outcomes, patient follow-up outcomes, or new field acquisitions. The workload charges are research weights. The acquisition study’s suffix order defines a retrospective experiment, and real capture chronology remains unknown. Prospective patient evidence would be needed to estimate useful automation and routine workflow effects.
